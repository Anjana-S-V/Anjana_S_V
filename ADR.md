# ADR: Product Management Portfolio Site

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-09-26

## 1. Context

We are building a portfolio website for a product management candidate. The portfolio is designed as a single long-page experience that tells a clear professional story across:

- work history
- product and project impact
- cross-functional experience
- strategic thinking and decision-making
- stakeholder communication
- role-fit for product management opportunities

This site is intended for hiring managers, PM interviewers, product leaders, and recruiters evaluating whether the candidate can operate in product roles with judgment, clarity, and execution ability.

The tone of the portfolio should be professional but not stiff, personal but not generic, and grounded in actual product thinking. It should avoid cliché phrases such as “passionate problem solver” or “results-driven professional” unless they are backed by clear evidence and real examples.

The portfolio should feel like a product manager telling a credible story about how they make decisions, work across teams, and ship outcomes — not like a standard resume written in generic corporate language.

## 2. Decision

We will build a single-page portfolio tailored specifically to product management, with:

- a clear PM-focused positioning statement
- a career timeline showing progression and product-related impact
- a curated project section highlighting product decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes
- filterable categories such as product strategy, execution, user research, growth, or cross-functional work
- anchor-link navigation for easy scanning
- a concise, clear narrative that connects experience to product management capability
- a contact section with relevant professional links and availability

The site will prioritize clarity, credibility, and product judgment over decorative branding or broad personal storytelling.

## 3. Why this decision

This approach fits the target role because product management is about showing how someone thinks, prioritizes, collaborates, and drives outcomes.

A single-page, timeline-based portfolio is well suited to that because it allows the candidate to demonstrate:

- progression from responsibility to ownership
- examples of product problem framing
- stakeholder management and cross-functional collaboration
- decision quality under ambiguity
- measurable impact from shipped work
- fit for the PM role without needing long-form narrative walls

This format is stronger than a generic portfolio because it makes the candidate’s product reasoning visible rather than just listing job titles and tools.

## 4. Explicitly out of scope

The following are intentionally not part of this project:

- multi-page portfolio architecture
- separate case-study pages for every project
- blog or long-form writing archive
- generic creative portfolio elements unrelated to product work
- a broad “about me” section full of personality fluff without evidence
- endless lists of tools and frameworks with no context
- a visual-heavy design that distracts from product thinking
- social-media-style branding or identity-first design choices without actual portfolio substance
- a broad personal brand site that tries to serve multiple audiences at once

This portfolio is not a general personal brand site. It is a focused product management portfolio designed to help hiring teams understand product capability and fit.

## 5. Alternatives considered and rejected

### A. Generic professional portfolio
Rejected because:

- it dilutes the candidate’s positioning for PM roles
- it reads like a broad resume instead of a product story
- it does not clearly show product judgment or decision-making
- it fails to differentiate the candidate from other applicants

### B. Full case-study archive with many subpages
Rejected because:

- adds navigation friction and makes review harder
- increases content complexity without improving clarity
- is not aligned with the single-page decision
- creates more maintenance and content burden than needed

### C. Creative-first portfolio with strong visual branding
Rejected because:

- product hiring teams care more about product thinking and outcomes than visual polish alone
- visual flair can distract from the substance of work
- the site should support the candidate’s PM narrative, not overshadow it

### D. Resume-only portfolio
Rejected because:

- resumes are concise but often lack evidence of judgment and product work
- PM roles require context, examples, and trade-off framing
- a portfolio should show more than responsibility lists

### E. Overly polished corporate language
Rejected because:

- it feels impersonal and unconvincing
- it hides the person behind generic language
- it weakens trust and reduces differentiation

## 6. Consequences

### Positive consequences

- clearer alignment with PM hiring expectations
- stronger differentiation from generic portfolio templates
- easier to connect work experience to product thinking
- more credible narrative for product leaders and recruiters
- concise, fast scanning experience
- better fit for a single-page deployment model

### Trade-offs

- the portfolio must be more selectively curated
- messaging needs to be sharper and more intentional
- PM-focused storytelling will require stronger examples and clearer impact language
- the tone must stay personal without becoming too informal or too vague

## 7. Summary

This ADR chooses a single-page, PM-focused portfolio because it is the clearest way to show product thinking, ownership, stakeholder collaboration, and outcomes in a format that hiring teams can review quickly. The portfolio will use a professional but approachable tone, avoid generic corporate clichés, and stay disciplined around product management relevance rather than broad personal branding. The project deliberately excludes generic portfolio patterns, blog-heavy content, and non-product-focused clutter because they do not support the target role or the intended narrative.
