03 / PRODUCT THINKING

How I approach a product problem.

My process is not a rigid framework. It is a way of moving from uncertainty toward a useful product while keeping users, constraints and learning in view.

01

Discover

Understand the user, context and the actual problem behind the initial request.

02

Define

Separate the core problem from noise and make the problem clear enough to work on.

03

Prioritize

Decide what matters now, what can wait, and which constraints should shape the first version.

04

Design

Turn the problem into the simplest useful experience that can be prototyped and tested.

05

Build

Work with technical constraints and turn the product idea into something real enough to learn from.

06

Learn

Use feedback, implementation constraints and observed behavior to decide what should change next.

04 / PRODUCT DECISIONS

The part of building I find most interesting: deciding what matters.

01

Simplify before adding.

In healthcare product work, I became more aware of how information overload can make a product harder to use. The question is often what to remove, not what else to add.

02

Build to learn.

Technical prototypes are useful when they expose product questions early — not only when they prove that something can be built.

03

AI needs a useful workflow.

A model output is not automatically a product. The surrounding interaction, context and user goal determine whether an AI feature is actually useful.
